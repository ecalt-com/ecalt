# Privacy notice — ECALT at [School name]

> **DRAFT for counsel review — not legal advice.** A plain-language notice for students, parents and staff. The school is the controller, so the school sends or publishes it (it's Annex D of the DPA). A shorter version for students is at the end. `[ ]` marks blanks.

**[School name]** uses **ECALT**, a learning service, for some lessons. This notice explains what information is used, why, and your choices. [School name] is responsible for your information; ECALT handles it for the school and only as the school instructs.

## What ECALT is used for

Teachers upload their own lesson material, and ECALT helps them turn it into step-by-step courses. Teachers check every course before students see it. Students work through the courses, answer short quizzes and can ask an AI tutor questions about the lesson.

## What information is used

**About students — only what's needed:**
- a display name (usually first name and last initial), grade and class, and optionally the school's own student number;
- a picture password or PIN for signing in (stored in scrambled form so nobody can read it) or a printed QR badge;
- progress through courses, quiz answers and scores;
- questions asked to the AI tutor and its answers;
- basic technical information needed to keep the service working and secure.

**ECALT does not collect students' email addresses, phone numbers, birth dates, photos or home addresses.**

**About staff:** name, school email, role, and a record of actions taken in the service.

## How it's used

- To show students their assigned courses, quizzes and tutor.
- To show teachers how their classes are doing.
- To keep the service secure and working.

It is **not** used for advertising, is **never sold**, and is **not used to build profiles** of students or to train AI models. Students don't receive marketing or "come back" messages.

## The AI tutor

When a student asks the tutor a question, the question and the lesson text are sent to an AI provider ([OpenAI / Anthropic]) to write the answer. The student's name isn't sent. The providers are not allowed to use this to train their AI.

**Teachers can read tutor conversations.** So that teachers can keep students safe and see where they need help, the student's class teachers and school administrators can read tutor conversations. Every time they do, it's recorded. The school can turn this off. `[If turned off at your school: "At our school, this is turned off."]`

## Who else handles the information

ECALT uses trusted service providers to run the service — hosting, database and storage, sign-in, email to staff, error monitoring and the AI providers above. They may be outside India. They may use the information only to provide their service to ECALT. `[Link to sub-processor list]`

## How long it's kept

While the student is at the school and using ECALT. At the end of the year the school may **archive** students (they can't sign in, but their progress is kept) or **delete** them. If the school stops using ECALT, everything is deleted after 30 days.

## Your choices and rights

Parents, students and staff can ask [School name] to see, correct or delete their information, or to stop using ECALT for a student — contact [school contact]. The school can export or delete a student's information at any time. You can also contact ECALT at support@ecalt.com, and we'll pass your request to the school. `[India: right to nominate, grievance officer and Data Protection Board complaint route — counsel to complete.]`

## Keeping it safe

Each school's information is kept separate from every other school's. Sign-in secrets are scrambled, repeated wrong guesses lock the account, and sessions end after 12 hours. Staff only see what their role allows, and ECALT reviews the service's security before each launch.

## Changes and contact

If this notice changes, [School name] will tell you. Questions: [school contact] or support@ecalt.com.
